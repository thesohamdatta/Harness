Manifesto

We need a third device.

A computer to create. A phone to connect. Neither was built to let you be present. Aura is the third device — worn, screenless, always aware.

The smartphone is a remarkable machine. It puts infinite knowledge in your pocket and connects you to everyone you know. But the same engineers who made it powerful also made it addictive. Notifications, infinite scrolls, attention algorithms — these are not bugs. They are the product.

We built Aura because we believe utility does not require distraction.

The problem

Every device we own demands something from us. Your phone lights up. Your computer dings. Your watch buzzes. Each interruption pulls you out of the moment and into a feed engineered to keep you there.

This is not a side effect. It is how the attention economy works. The longer you stay, the more they earn. And AI is not going to fix this — it will accelerate it, unless we build something different.

Why Aura

Aura does not have a screen. It cannot show you an ad, play a video, or load an infinite feed. It listens, it sees, it remembers — and it leaves your attention where it belongs: on the world around you.

This is the least socially disruptive device we have ever built. No notifications. No pings. No doomscrolling. Just a quiet intelligence that works for you, not for someone else's revenue.

Less distraction. More done.

A personal computer to create. A smartphone to connect. Aura to be present.

That is why we need a third device.
